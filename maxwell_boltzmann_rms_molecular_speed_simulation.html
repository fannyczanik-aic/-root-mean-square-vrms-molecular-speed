Probability density vs. molecular speed (v in m/s)

vp vavg vrms

Because vrms = √(v̄²) averages the squares of velocities, faster molecules in the high-speed tail carry greater weight, ensuring vp < vavg < vrms.

√T

Temperature Dependence: vrms ∝ √T

Molecular speed is directly proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature. Heating a gas sample to four times its absolute temperature (4T) doubles the characteristic speed (2vrms) of its particles and broadens the distribution curve.

1/√M

Mass Dependence: vrms ∝ √(1/M)

At any shared temperature, all ideal gases have the same average translational kinetic energy (½mv̄² = ³/₂kBT). Consequently, molecular speed is inversely proportional to the square root of molar mass—lighter molecules move significantly faster on average than heavier ones.
